## What a product key actually buys

Microsoft sells two different things under the Office name, and the difference decides how much you'll spend over the next few years.

**Office 2024 is a one-time purchase.** You pay a single up-front cost and get the Office apps for one computer, PC or Mac. Microsoft states that one-time purchases "will not receive any new features and don't have an upgrade option." When the next major release arrives, you buy again. Nothing about the license changes if you keep using it; you simply stay on the version you bought.

**Microsoft 365 is a subscription.** Microsoft describes it as a service that "ensures you always have the most up-to-date Microsoft 365 apps." On Family, Premium and Pro plans you can share the subscription with up to five other people, install on all your devices, and stay signed in on five at the same time across PC, Mac, tablet and phone. Updates, fixes and new features arrive as part of the subscription.

There is also a free path that most price-comparison pages never mention. Microsoft 365 Online runs in a browser at no cost, and Microsoft's own wording is that you can do "almost everything" with the online apps, though "some more advanced features are only available with the Microsoft 365 or Office desktop apps." If your work is light editing and sharing, try the browser version before spending anything.

The one-time license isn't a stripped-down product, either. ## What the price spread is telling you

Two numbers in that table deserve a closer look, because they're the ones buyers misread most often.

**The same product name, two very different prices.** Office Home & Business 2024 appears at $94.85 from Mr Key Shop and $180.00 from ProductKeys. These are separate sellers listing what may well be different license types, and the listings don't spell out the difference. Don't average them and don't treat either one as "the real price." The only safe reading is that the name on a listing doesn't guarantee the same rights as the name on another listing.

**Struck-through prices are the seller's own.** Several listings show "original price $99.90" next to a current price of $13.90 or $19.90. Nothing in the material reviewed indicates that $99.90 is a Microsoft suggested retail price. It's a reference figure the seller set for itself. Judge the current price, not the discount.

The Pro Plus range from $9.90 to $30.00 looks like random markups but mostly tracks two things: the version year (2024, 2021, 2019, 2016, 2013, 2010) and the license label the seller attaches, whether that's LTSC, MAK or Retail. Those labels describe different licensing arrangements; the listings use them without explanation, and how each one affects your rights is something the seller should be able to answer before you pay. A 2024 LTSC key at $9.99 and a 2024 Professional Plus key at $19.90 are not the same purchase, and a 2010 key at $9.90 is not a bargain.

## How to redeem a Microsoft Office product key

Microsoft publishes the redemption path in two places, and the steps are short enough that most people finish in a few minutes.

1. Go to **setup.office.com**, or **office.com/setup**, or **Microsoft365.com/setup**. All three are official entry points Microsoft lists.
2. Sign in with your Microsoft account, or create one. Microsoft's instruction is explicit: remember this account, because it lets you "install or reinstall Office later, without a product key."
3. Enter the product key **without hyphens** if you're prompted for it. Keys are 25 characters. If no input box appears, the key was filled in automatically.
4. Follow the prompts to finish redemption, then download and install.

Where your key physically lives depends on how you bought it. In a sealed retail box, the key is inside the packaging. Bought from an online retailer, it may arrive by email or be printed on the receipt. Microsoft's note is that "you only need to redeem this key once."

Three special cases:

- **Bought from Microsoft Store:** sign in at microsoftstore.com, click your name in the top right, open Order History, find the one-time Office purchase, and select Install Office to display the key. That step shows the key; it doesn't install anything.
- **Microsoft 365 Business Standard:** redeem at signup.microsoft.com/get-started/setupkey, again without hyphens.
- **Renewing Microsoft 365:** sign in with the Microsoft account tied to the existing subscription, not a new one.

## Why a cheap key can stop working

Microsoft's product key support page is unusually direct about this, and it's worth reading in full before you hand over money to a marketplace seller:

> If your Office product key doesn't work, or has stopped working, you should contact the seller and request a refund. If you bought a product key separate from the software, it's very possible the product key was stolen or otherwise fraudulently obtained, and subsequently blocked for use.

The same page continues: there are "many dishonest sellers who offer stolen, abused, or otherwise unauthorized Microsoft product keys for sale," sometimes printed on counterfeit labels or cards with a download link, and sometimes distributed with media that isn't authorized for resale, such as promotional media or manufacturer reinstallation media.

Note the wording: *very possible*, not certain. A cheap key is not automatically fraudulent, and it is not automatically safe. What you're doing at $10–$20 is accepting a risk in exchange for the price, and the person who absorbs that risk if it goes wrong is you, not the seller. Microsoft's recommended remedy is to go back to the seller and ask for a refund, which puts the seller's refund terms ahead of the sticker price in importance. Before paying, find out whether the seller refunds a key that fails to activate, and whether they'll replace it.

## The version trap: cheap keys for software that's already retired

Microsoft's key redemption page carries a note that a lot of resale listings quietly ignore: **support has ended for Office 2021, Office 2019 and Office 2016.**

Those versions are still on sale at low prices. Office 2021 Professional Plus is listed at $13.90, Office 2019 Professional Plus at $10.99, Office 2016 Professional Plus at $9.99, Office 2013 at $9.90 and Office 2010 at $9.90. Even if a key activates cleanly, you'd be running software Microsoft no longer supports, with no security updates and no path forward except buying again.

The same logic applies to Office Home & Student 2016 at $12.90 and Office Home & Student 2019 at $21.90. Both are cheaper than a current license. Both are past support. For a machine that never touches the internet, that might be an acceptable trade. For anything connected, it's a false saving.

## FAQ

**Do I need my product key again if I reinstall Office?**
No. Once the key is redeemed to a Microsoft account, you sign in with that account and install. Microsoft's instructions state you can install or reinstall later without a product key. This is the strongest argument for redeeming to an account you'll still control in three years.

**Can I install one key on two computers?**
The one-time Office Home 2024 license is sold for one PC or Mac. If two machines need Office at the same time, the subscription is the cleaner route: Microsoft 365 Family, Premium and Pro allow installation across your devices with five simultaneous sign-ins.

**Where exactly do I type the key?**
At setup.office.com, office.com/setup or Microsoft365.com/setup, after signing in. Enter it without hyphens. If you don't see a key field, it has already been filled in for you.

**Will Office 2024 upgrade itself to the next version?**
No. Microsoft states that one-time purchases receive no new features and have no upgrade option; moving to the next major release means buying it again. If staying current matters to you, that's the subscription's whole purpose.

**What do I do if the key fails?**
Contact the seller and request a refund, which is Microsoft's own advice. A standalone key that fails may have been stolen or fraudulently obtained and blocked, and no amount of retrying will fix that.
